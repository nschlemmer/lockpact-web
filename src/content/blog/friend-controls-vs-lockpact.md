Friend Controls and LockPact are the two apps closest to actually being in the same category: both replace a self-controlled screen time limit with a real person deciding whether you get more time. That's rare — most "screen time" apps are still just you versus a setting. (For the wider field, including a couple of apps that only sound like they belong here, see our roundup of [apps that let a friend control your screen time](/blog/apps-that-let-a-friend-control-your-screen-time/).)

But "a friend is involved" is where the similarity ends. The two apps make fundamentally different bets about who that friend should be and what power they should have.

## How Friend Controls actually works

You set your own daily limits per app. When you hit a limit, Friend Controls locks you out and lets you send a request to one or more friends you've invited as "Controllers." They see the request and tap approve or deny. You can invite multiple Controllers — it's not tied to one specific person.

Blocking a single app is free. Blocking more than one app requires a paid tier: Friend Controls offers weekly ($4.99), monthly, and yearly subscriptions (roughly $3–$60 depending on term and plan), plus a $29.99 lifetime purchase, per its current App Store listing. It's iOS only, built by developer Ryan Moazzami, sitting at 4.7 stars across 515 ratings as of this writing — a real, established app, not a ghost listing.

## How LockPact actually works

LockPact starts from the opposite direction. Instead of you setting your own limit and asking a friend for an extension when you break it, you and a partner pair up and each lock apps *on each other's* phones from the start. There's no moment where you're negotiating your own rule with yourself — the lock is already theirs to hold. When you want back in, you send an unlock request and your partner approves or declines it. It works the same way in reverse for whatever they've locked on your phone.

It's free — the core mutual-lock-and-approve mechanic isn't a teaser for a subscription, it's the whole app. There's an optional one-time "Support LockPact" tip and a rewarded ad shown before some unlock requests, but no recurring paywall gates the core feature. LockPact also detects if you turn off Screen Time access to sidestep the lock entirely and notifies your partner when that happens — Friend Controls' public materials don't describe an equivalent bypass-detection mechanism.

## Where Friend Controls genuinely wins

Be honest about this part: Friend Controls fits some people better than LockPact does, and it's not close for those people.

**You don't have one specific partner.** If your accountability circle is a friend group rather than a single significant other or roommate, Friend Controls' multiple-Controller model is built for that. LockPact is designed around one paired partner; it's not the tool for "any of my five friends can approve this."

**You want to set your own limits, not have someone else define them.** Friend Controls keeps you in charge of the initial rule — you decide what counts as too much Instagram. The friend only gets involved once you've already broken your own limit. If having a partner set the lock from the start feels like too much control to hand over, Friend Controls' lighter-touch version is the better fit.

**Its track record is longer.** 515 ratings and a mature feature set (website blocking, time-period rules, app groups) mean Friend Controls has been through more real-world usage and iteration than a newer entrant.

## Where LockPact wins

**You want it to actually be mutual.** Friend Controls' Controllers approve your requests, but nobody's approving theirs — there's no symmetric obligation built into the app. LockPact's model means your partner is holding a lock too, so the accountability runs both ways, not just toward you.

**You don't want to pay to block more than one app.** LockPact's core feature — mutual locking with unlock approval — has no per-app paywall. Friend Controls charges once you go past a single blocked app, which for most people (Instagram *and* TikTok *and* whatever else) is immediately.

**You care about bypass detection.** LockPact watches for the "just turn off Screen Time access" move and tells your partner when it happens. That's the difference between an app that only works when you're already cooperating and one that surfaces the moment you stop.

## Choose them if / choose us if

**Choose Friend Controls if:** you want a friend-group-style safety net rather than one dedicated partner, you're fine setting your own limits and only escalating on overage, and you don't mind paying to cover more than one app.

**Choose LockPact if:** you want one partner mutually locked in with you (not just approving your requests), you want the full feature free with no per-app paywall, and bypass detection matters to you.

Neither is objectively "better" — they're built for different accountability shapes. If you're not sure which shape fits, the honest test is: do you already have one person in mind you'd trust to hold your lock and have you hold theirs? If yes, that's LockPact. If it's more of a rotating group of friends you'd ping when you're out of Instagram time, that's Friend Controls.

## A note on Friend Controls' free app

Friend Controls' "block one app free" line is worth sitting with for a second, because it exposes the real product decision behind both apps. For Friend Controls, the free tier is a taste — enough to prove the mechanic works, not enough to actually cover the two or three apps most people actually struggle with. That's a normal freemium shape, and there's nothing wrong with it; it's how most of the App Store works.

LockPact made a different bet: the mutual lock isn't a lead-in to a subscription, because there isn't one to lead into. The apps you and your partner lock — however many — are covered from the first pairing. The tradeoff is the opposite one: LockPact is newer, with a shorter track record than Friend Controls' 515 ratings, and it only works if you have one person willing to pair with you specifically, not a loose circle of Controllers.
