Long-distance couples don't need one app. They need a few, each doing one job: keeping in touch, sharing a calendar, watching something together, and agreeing how much of the day goes to the phone. Most apps below are free to download, and [LockPact](/go/b-long-distance-relationship-apps/), the one built for phone boundaries, is free to use. Prices were checked on App Store listings on October 15, 2026.

## What are the best apps for long-distance couples?

The best apps for a long-distance couple are the ones you'll both open: Between or Tethered for staying in touch, Paired for daily questions, OurCal for a shared calendar, FaceTime SharePlay for watching together, and LockPact for phone boundaries. Pick one per job, not one of everything. Two people juggling nine apps is a long-distance problem of its own.

## Which apps help you stay in touch?

For staying in touch, Between is the steadier all-rounder, with a private chat, shared photos and a calendar, and Tethered suits couples who want small daily moments and games. Both are free to download with paid upgrades, so try the free versions together for a week before you pay for anything.

### Between

Between is a private messenger for two, with shared photos, a calendar and an anniversary counter. Its listing shows about 21,000 ratings at 4.8. Between Plus lists $2.99 a month, $24.99 a year or $26.99 for lifetime, and the base app is free.

### Tethered

Tethered is built for couples who spend time apart: small daily moments, drawings and photos sent to your partner's home screen, a map and the distance between you. Its listing shows monthly at $12.99 and yearly at $39.99 with a 7-day trial.

### Loveit

Loveit leans on widgets: a live photo on your partner's home screen, plus hugs and nudges sent from a tap. Its Couple Premium lists monthly at $11.99 and yearly at $29.99 among several price points.

## Which apps give you something to talk about?

For conversation, Paired asks you both a daily question and takes about five minutes a day, which suits calls that have gone flat. InnerBond is a newer option with daily questions and games. Both are free to download with paid tiers, so start with the free versions.

### Paired

Paired asks you both a daily question and builds from it in about five minutes a day. It's the most-reviewed app here, with over 200,000 ratings. Premium lists from $14.99 a month, with annual prices from $39.99.

### InnerBond

InnerBond is the newest app on this list: daily questions, games and a shared streak, aimed at long-distance couples. At the time we checked it had one rating, so treat it as promising, not proven.

## How do you share a calendar across time zones?

Use a shared calendar where each event has its own chat, so nobody has to do the time-zone maths in their head. OurCal does that: every event has its own chat and changes post automatically. It's free to download, and its paid sharing plans start at $6.99.

### OurCal

OurCal is a shared calendar with a messenger built in: every event has its own chat, and changes post automatically. It's free to download, and its paid sharing plans start at $6.99. For a couple, a calendar that shows both of your local times is worth more than another chat app.

## How do you watch things together?

FaceTime's SharePlay is the free place to start: it's built into iPhone, and during a call you open the More menu and choose SharePlay. Each of you needs your own access to the title. Teleparty is a paid option for some streaming sites if SharePlay doesn't cover your service.

### FaceTime SharePlay

SharePlay is free and built into iPhone. During a FaceTime call, open the More menu and choose SharePlay, or start from a supported app like Apple TV. Apple says playback is shared, so either of you can pause or rewind, and each person needs their own access to the title.

### Teleparty

Teleparty syncs playback on some streaming sites and adds group chat. Premium is $6.59 a month or $49.99 a year on its listing. If you both already have the same subscription, SharePlay is the simpler first try.

## How do you set phone boundaries when you're far apart?

The honest reason long-distance couples need this: the phone is the relationship. It's where you talk, and it's also where Instagram, TikTok and the group chat live. Evenings that were meant for a call turn into two people half-watching a screen.

## How many of these do you actually need?

Fewer than you think. Use one app for each thing you'd otherwise argue about: a calendar if time zones cause missed calls, a question app if calls feel flat, a phone boundary if screens interrupt them. Every extra app is one more notification competing for the attention you're trying to give each other.

Apps that solve a problem you don't have make a long-distance relationship feel like more work, not less.

Also decide who pays. Most of the apps above charge for the features that make them useful, and two subscriptions of $40 a year each add up fast across two people. Choose one account per couple where the app allows it, and check whether your partner needs a paid plan too before you commit.

## Which should you start with?

Start with one chat or question app you'll both open, SharePlay for movie nights, and a phone boundary you agree on together. Add a shared calendar when time zones start causing missed calls. Skip anything either of you stops opening after two weeks; an app nobody uses is just a subscription.
