Every iPhone can lock or block an app several ways, all free, all built in, and all honest about one limit: you can turn each off yourself. This guide covers **every built-in method on iOS 27 with exact steps and its real off-switch**, so you can pick the right one, then explains why they all fail the same way and what to do about it. If you want a lock that someone else has to approve, [LockPact](/go/b-how-to-lock-apps-on-iphone/) is a free-to-use iOS app where your partner decides when you get back in.

## What is the difference between locking and blocking an app?

Locking an app means opening it needs Face ID, a fingerprint or your passcode. Blocking an app means Screen Time stops you using it, by schedule or by an allowance. Locking protects an app from other people picking up your phone. Blocking is for your own habits. Most people searching "lock apps" mean one, and most people searching "block apps" mean the other.

Apple's names are the clearest guide: its iPhone guide calls the first "Lock or hide an app", and the second Screen Time. We use both words on purpose, and "block" is also what people type into search, so we use it for Apple's own features and the searches that lead here.

## How do you lock an app on iPhone?

Touch and hold the app's icon on your Home Screen, tap **Require Face ID** (or Touch ID or Passcode), then tap it again to lock the app. From then on the app needs your face, finger or passcode to open, and it locks again every time you leave it. Apple says information inside a locked app also stays out of notification previews, search, Siri suggestions and your call history.

1. On the Home Screen, touch and hold the app until the Quick Actions menu opens.
2. Tap **Require Face ID** (or **Touch ID** or **Passcode**).
3. Tap **Require Face ID** again to lock it. To open it later, authenticate each time.

That's Apple's built-in app lock, and it protects the app from someone picking up your phone. It doesn't protect the app from you.

### What can't be locked this way?

Apple's iOS 27 guide says apps that come installed can't be hidden. Its earlier guides listed Calculator, Camera and Settings as apps that can't be locked this way; the iOS 27 guide doesn't repeat that list, so check on your phone. If you wanted to lock Settings so nobody can change your Screen Time rules, this isn't the tool. A Screen Time passcode is.

### How do you hide an app?

Touch and hold the icon, tap **Hide and Require Face ID**, authenticate, then tap **Hide App**. The app leaves your Home Screen and moves to a **Hidden** folder at the bottom of App Library, which also needs authentication to open. To see a list of hidden apps, go to **Settings → Apps → Hidden Apps**. Apple says the locked or hidden state stays on that iPhone and doesn't sync through iCloud.

**The off-switch:** touch and hold the app (or open it from the Hidden folder), tap **Don't Require Face ID**, and authenticate. That's you, with your own face.

## How do you block apps on iPhone with Screen Time?

On iOS 27, open Settings, tap Screen Time, and choose the tool that matches what you want. Screen Time Schedule sets hours when only the apps you allow are available, Time Allowances set daily time per category, Apps & Websites blocks specific sites, and Content & Privacy Restrictions controls content and features. Apple calls the old App Limits and Downtime by these new names.

1. Open **Settings → Screen Time**.
2. Pick the tool: **Screen Time Schedule**, **Time Allowances**, **Apps & Websites**, or **Content & Privacy Restrictions**.
3. Set it up as the sections below describe.

### Time Allowances (App Limits on iOS 26)

Time Allowances give a category of apps a daily budget. Open **Settings → Screen Time → Time Allowances**, drag a slider to set your total, or tap a category and drag its slider. Tap **Manage Categories** to see which apps sit in each category. Apple says you can use apps in a category until they reach the category's allowance or the daily total. **The off-switch:** change or delete the allowance from the same screen, with the Screen Time passcode if one is set.

**On iOS 26 and earlier:** it's **App Limits → Add Limit**. Apple's note for those versions: by default, Screen Time limits can be ignored once reached.

### Screen Time Schedule (Downtime on iOS 26)

A schedule is hours when most apps are off. Open **Settings → Screen Time → Screen Time Schedule**, turn on **Schedule**, tap **Add Schedule**, choose the days, and set time windows. Each window shows which Time Allowance categories are available. **The off-switch:** turn off Schedule or delete a time window on the same screen, so again it's whoever knows the passcode.

![The Screen Time Schedule row highlighted on the Screen Time screen](../../assets/screens/how-to-lock-apps-on-iphone/05-open-schedule.webp)

**On iOS 26 and earlier:** it's **Downtime**. With a Screen Time passcode set, **Block at Downtime** makes restricted apps unavailable instead of dimmed.

### Always Allowed

Always Allowed lists the apps and contacts that stay available regardless of any schedule or allowance. Open **Settings → Screen Time → Always Allowed** and tap **Add Apps** or **Add People**. It's the exception list, so check it when a limit "isn't working": an app in this list is never limited.

### Apps & Websites and Content & Privacy Restrictions

Apps & Websites lets you block specific websites, and Content & Privacy Restrictions controls content and built-in features. On iOS 27, **Settings → Screen Time → Content & Privacy Restrictions** lets you allow or block content like Books, Movies, TV Shows and Music, and **Other Features** lets you allow or block things like AirDrop and Camera. **The off-switch:** flip it back, with the passcode if one is set.

## How do you lock yourself into one app?

Guided Access is the odd one out: instead of locking apps away, it locks you in to one app. Go to **Settings → Accessibility → Guided Access**, turn it on, and set a passcode. Open the app, triple-click the side button, and tap **Start**. To end a session, triple-click the side button again, authenticate with your Guided Access passcode or Face ID, and tap **End**. It's for handing your phone over for one app, or a focused stretch of reading.

You can set a time limit, disable touch on parts of the screen, and block the keyboard. Apple notes Crash Detection and Emergency Services aren't available during a session, so end it to make an emergency call. **The off-switch:** the Guided Access passcode, which you chose.

## Why does every built-in lock fail the same way?

Because you know the code. Require Face ID opens for your face. Time Allowances, Schedules and Content & Privacy Restrictions all unlock with the Screen Time passcode, and by default you set that passcode. The person who needs the limit is the person who can remove it, usually at 11pm when you're tired and your thumb is already moving.

No setting changes that completely. A device owner can always get around an iPhone restriction, and anyone who tells you otherwise is overclaiming. What changes the outcome is who holds the code. If your partner or a friend you trust sets the Screen Time passcode and you don't know it, turning off a limit stops being a tap and becomes a conversation.

## Which built-in method should you use?

Pick by the problem. If your worry is other people on your phone, lock or hide the app. If your worry is yourself, use Screen Time, and accept that you hold the code unless you hand it to someone. The table summarizes who can turn each one off.

| Method | What it stops | Who can turn it off | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Require Face ID | Others opening one app | You, with Face ID | Free |
| Hide app | Others finding one app | You, with Face ID | Free |
| Time Allowances (App Limits on iOS 26) | Your daily use of a category | Whoever knows the Screen Time passcode (you, by default) | Free |
| Screen Time Schedule (Downtime on iOS 26) | Your use during set hours | Whoever knows the Screen Time passcode (you, by default) | Free |
| Content & Privacy Restrictions | Selected content and features | Whoever knows the Screen Time passcode (you, by default) | Free |
| Guided Access | Leaving one app during a session | The Guided Access passcode you set | Free |
