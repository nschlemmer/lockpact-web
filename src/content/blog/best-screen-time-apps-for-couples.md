## What "screen time app for couples" actually means

Before comparing, it's worth separating three different things that all get marketed under "couples + screen time":

1. **Synchronized self-blocking.** Both partners set their own limits, and the app lets you schedule a shared window (dinner, date night) where both phones lock at once. You're still each controlling your own device — the app just makes it easy to do it in sync.
2. **Shared visibility, no control.** You can see each other's habit or usage stats, maybe cheer each other on, but neither of you can actually unlock or lock the other's apps.
3. **Mutual key-holding.** Each partner locks apps on their own phone, and only the *other* partner can approve unlocking them. This is the model with actual teeth — the decision to let you back in isn't yours to make alone.

Most "couples screen time" tools live in category 1 or 2. Category 3 is much rarer, and it's where the real accountability lives, because your ability to undo the block depends on someone else, not your own willpower at 11pm.

## The roundup

### LockPact — mutual key-holding, free

LockPact is built around category 3: you each pick apps to lock on your own phone, and only your partner can approve your unlock requests (and you approve theirs, if you've paired). Neither person is the "controller" — pairing creates two locks, one per person, from the moment you connect. If you turn off Screen Time access to sidestep your own lock, the app detects it and notifies your partner. It's free with no subscription tier — an optional one-time Support LockPact tip and a rewarded ad shown before some unlock requests are how it sustains itself, not a paywall on the core feature. iOS only, and — worth repeating — it works identically for any two people, not just romantic partners.

### Jolt — synchronized blocking with a dedicated couples page

[Jolt](https://www.thejoltapp.com/couples-use-case) is a real screen time app with its own couples use-case page, and it's honest to call it out specifically since it's one of the few competitors actually targeting this search. The mechanic is synchronized self-blocking: you can sync blocking and app limits during dinners or dates, and run "Partner Challenges" where both of you disconnect together. That's category 1 above — each person is still locking their own phone on a shared schedule, not holding a key to the other's. Jolt markets "hard blocks that don't negotiate" (rules can't be edited once activated) and includes an "Attention Score" tracking focus, sleep, and discipline. 4.4 stars, 96 ratings, iOS 17+. Pricing is a maze of tiers — weekly $5.99–$7.99, monthly $11.99–$19.99, yearly $69.99–$99.99, or a $79.99 lifetime option, plus a separate $3.00 "Hard Mode SOS" purchase — verified against its live App Store listing.

### Habi — shared habit tracking with screen time blocking bundled in

[Habi](https://habi.app/) is primarily a habit tracker, not a screen time app, but it bundles in screen time blocking during focus sessions and lets you invite a partner (or friends) into shared projects to track progress and send gentle reminders. That's category 2: visibility and encouragement, not unlock control — your partner can see you're falling behind, but they're not the one deciding whether your blocked apps open back up. Free to start, with an optional Pro upgrade ($1.99–$89.99 depending on tier). Apple only, so it's a non-starter if your partner is on Android.

### A note on Cupla

If you've seen Cupla mentioned alongside couples screen-time tools, it's worth a correction: Cupla is a shared couples calendar app (500k+ couples, 4.7 stars) — to-do lists, a date-night planner, a private chat. It doesn't block apps or manage screen time at all. Great tool if you want a joint calendar; not a screen time app, so it's not really a fair comparison here.

### For contrast: solo tools with a "buddy" bolted on

Opal, one sec, and ScreenZen are strong solo friction tools, and some let you add an "accountability buddy" who gets notified when you use a blocked app — but that buddy doesn't approve anything, they're just informed. If the whole point is that your partner has real say over your unlock, none of the three put them in that seat. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is there a free screen time app for couples?

Yes. LockPact is free with no subscription — the mutual lock-and-approve mechanic is the whole product. Jolt and Habi are also free to start but paywall meaningful parts of the experience (blocking flexibility, extra tiers) behind a subscription.

### What's the difference between a couples screen time app and a regular screen time app with sharing?

A regular screen time app usually just adds visibility — your partner can see your stats or get notified when you go over a limit, but the decision to unlock stays entirely with you. A true couples screen time app gives your partner an actual say: either synchronized blocking (Jolt's model, where you're both locked on the same schedule) or mutual key-holding (LockPact's model, where your partner approves your specific unlock requests).

### Do both partners need to install the same app?

For every app in this comparison, yes. The synchronization, shared visibility, or unlock approval all happen inside the app itself, so both people need it installed for the couples feature to actually function.

### Can I use these apps if I'm not in a romantic relationship?

Yes — and you should read that as the default, not an exception. LockPact, Jolt, and Habi all work the same way for roommates, close friends, or siblings as they do for romantic partners. "Couples" is just the term most people search when they're picturing a two-person accountability setup; the mechanics don't require a relationship label.

### Does bypassing these apps just mean turning Screen Time off in Settings?

For any app built on Apple's Screen Time API, yes — the device owner can revoke access through Settings and Face ID in a few taps, and no third-party app can prevent that; it's a platform limit, not a bug in any one app. What varies is whether the app notices. LockPact detects the bypass and notifies your partner when it happens.
