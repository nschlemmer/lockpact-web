## What makes a good screen time app?

A good screen time app gets you to change a habit you've already decided to change. Three things decide whether it does: how much friction it adds (a pause, a task, a lock), who can turn it off, and whether you'll keep using it. Most apps below let you turn them off yourself, which is why the "who can turn it off" column matters more than the feature list.

This guide is for adults managing their own screen time. The top results for "best screen time app" are mostly parental control lists, which is a different job. If you're setting limits for a child, use Family Sharing and Apple's parental controls.

## Which apps are built into iPhone?

Apple's Screen Time is built in and free. On iOS 27 you open **Settings → Screen Time** and choose **Screen Time Schedule** for hours when only the apps you allow are available, **Time Allowances** for a daily budget per category (Entertainment, Games, Social Media), or **Always Allowed** for apps that stay available. In iOS 27, Screen Time no longer lists App Limits or Downtime; Apple's iOS 27 guide describes these instead.

### Apple Screen Time

It's the right place to start: free, no account, and it shows you where the time goes. The weakness is that you set the passcode and know it, so you can undo any setting. Apple's own guide lets you add a passcode through **Manage Screen Time → Lock Screen Time Settings**, and it only helps if someone other than you holds it. ## Which apps add friction instead of a lock?

Three apps slow you down rather than stop you. They work for people who already want to change and need a nudge.

### one sec

one sec puts a deliberate pause before an app opens and asks if you really want it. Essentials are free and Pro is $19.99 a year on its listing. You can walk through the pause, which is the point: it's about noticing, not forcing. See [one sec vs. LockPact](/blog/onesec-vs-lockpact/).

### ScreenZen

ScreenZen adds friction delays before the apps you choose, with optional streak sharing with friends. Its listing shows it as free, with tips as the only in-app purchases. It's friction, not a lock, and you can turn it off. See [ScreenZen vs. LockPact](/blog/screenzen-vs-lockpact/).

### Clearspace

Clearspace asks you to do something, such as pushups, before an app opens, and can notify a partner when you go over budget. That notification is visibility, not approval. Premium is $44.99 a year. Our [Clearspace review](/blog/clearspace-app-review/) goes through the accountability feature.

## Which apps schedule blocking for you?

These apps lock your chosen apps on a schedule. You set the rules, so you can change them.

### Opal

Opal is the best-designed of the group, with scheduled sessions, and its free plan allows one rule on iOS, Android and Mac. Pro is $99.99 a year, $19.99 a month, or $399 lifetime, per opalapp.com. Comparison: [Opal vs. LockPact](/blog/opal-vs-lockpact/).

### Jomo

Jomo blocks apps for times you set and lets you take mindful breaks. Plus is $29.99 a year or $99.99 lifetime on its listing. It's the cheapest of the scheduled blockers after AppBlock.

### AppBlock

AppBlock has the most knobs: schedules, location rules, timers and Strict Mode, which protects its own toggle but not Apple's Settings route. Premium is $4.99 a month or $29.99 a year. See [AppBlock vs. LockPact](/blog/appblock-vs-lockpact/).

### Freedom

Freedom blocks apps and websites during sessions across your devices. The free plan has no scheduling or Locked Mode; Premium is $3.33 a month billed yearly, $8.99 monthly, or $99.50 lifetime, per freedom.to. See [Freedom vs. LockPact](/blog/freedom-vs-lockpact/).

### BePresent

BePresent mixes app blocking with rewards and competition with friends. Its listing has about 67,500 ratings at 4.8, the most of any app here after Opal, and Pro lists from $24.99 a year.

## Which screen time app should you choose?

If you just want to see where your time goes, use Apple's Screen Time. If you want a nudge, try one sec or ScreenZen. If you want rules that run on a schedule, pick Opal, Jomo or AppBlock. If you've set limits before and tapped through every time, you need the off-switch to belong to someone else: a Screen Time passcode a partner holds, or LockPact. No app makes quitting impossible; the best ones make it slower, more visible and a conversation.
