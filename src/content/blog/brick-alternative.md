If you want what Brick does without buying and carrying a device, there are app-only options, and one of them is free. [LockPact](/go/b-brick-alternative/) is a free iOS app where a partner, not a gadget, holds the key. This guide covers **five app-only Brick alternatives, what each costs, and the honest answer to the only question that matters: who can turn it off?**

## Is Brick worth it?

Brick is worth it if you'll reliably keep the device within reach and want a physical off-switch. It's $59 once with no subscription, according to its site, and works with iPhones on iOS 18 or later. It's a poor fit if you travel, forget it, or need a lock another person controls. The alternatives below serve those people.

### What does the Brick app and device do?

Brick is a physical device that blocks apps on your phone. You pick the apps in its app, tap your phone against the device to start a session, and tap it again to end one. It comes with five Emergency Unbricks that unblock all apps if you lose the device, and a 30-day money-back guarantee. We checked all of this on getbrick.com on October 15, 2026.

### Who is Brick good for?

People who want the off-switch to be somewhere they have to walk to. That friction is real, and a software toggle can't match it. If you keep it by the front door or on a desk and you want to stop yourself on impulse, Brick does that job well, and the price is a one-off, not a yearly charge.

### Who should look elsewhere?

If you forget it at home, the lock goes with it. If you travel, you either carry it or go without. If you lose it, you're down to the five emergency unbricks. And if the real problem is that you override your own rules, a device you control still has you holding the key. None of this is a knock on Brick. It's a reason some people want an alternative.

## What are the hardware options: Brick, Bloom and Unpluq?

Brick, Bloom and Unpluq are the three hardware locks people compare. Bloom is the cheapest at $39 for the card, Brick is $59 once, and Unpluq needs a subscription on top of its $26.50 tag. All three start a lock when you tap your phone to a physical object. They differ in price, in emergency exits and in how they handle the day you don't have the object.

| If you forget or lose it | Five Emergency Unbricks | Three emergency exits, plus one more each month | Not stated on its home page; check its FAQ |

| Guarantee | 30 days | 30 days | 30 days |

Prices are from each company's own store on October 16, 2026. For a head-to-head on the two most-compared, see [Brick vs. Bloom](/blog/brick-vs-bloom/).

## What happens when you forget the puck?

You use an emergency exit and then you run out. Brick lists five Emergency Unbricks and Bloom lists three, plus one a month. Unpluq's home page doesn't say, so check its FAQ before you buy. When the exits are gone and the device is at home, the lock has nothing to end it with, which is the single situation hardware locks handle worst.

That's the design trade-off. A physical object gives you real friction on a normal day and no good answer on a bad one: travel, a lost key, a forgotten bag. App-only locks flip it. They're always with you, but the off-switch is a software setting. Choose by which failure you can live with.

## Why look for a Brick alternative with no hardware?

People leave the device question for the same four reasons. They forget it at home, so the lock goes with it. They travel and it doesn't. They lose it, and then rely on the emergency unbricks. Or they don't want to pay $59 for something they're not sure they'll keep using.

Those are good reasons. The catch is that giving up the hardware means giving up the physical barrier, and every app on this list has to replace it with something else. The something else is what separates them.

### 1. LockPact: the partner is the brick

LockPact puts another person where Brick puts a device. You and a partner each choose apps to lock on your own phones, and neither of you can unlock without the other approving. If either of you bypasses it in Settings, which Apple's design always allows, the other person is told right away.

That's the part a drawer can't do: your partner doesn't get left at home, and asking them for a favor costs something a tap doesn't. It needs a second person who's willing, and it's free to use; the only optional purchases are tips and Willpower Waivers, which skip the short video before an unlock request. If you'd rather start solo, LockPact has a solo mode with timers, and you can add a partner when you're ready. [Get LockPact on the App Store](/go/b-brick-alternative/).

### 2. Opal: the polished solo option

Opal is the best-designed solo blocker here, with scheduled sessions and a free plan limited to one rule. Pro adds unlimited rules and a stricter mode for $99.99 a year (opalapp.com/pricing). Its off-switch is still you, since you can revoke its access in Settings; the exception is a partner who holds your Screen Time passcode. We compare it in detail in [Opal vs. LockPact](/blog/opal-vs-lockpact/).

### 3. AppBlock: granular rules

AppBlock has the most knobs: schedules, location rules, timers and Strict Mode. Premium is $4.99 a month or $29.99 a year on its App Store listing. Strict Mode protects its own in-app toggle but not Apple's Settings route, so it raises the bar without closing the door. See [AppBlock vs. LockPact](/blog/appblock-vs-lockpact/).

### 4. Clearspace: a task before the app opens

Clearspace's signature is making you do something before an app opens, such as pushups, and it offers accountability partners who are notified when you go over budget. That's visibility, not approval: they can see it happened but can't stand between you and the app. Premium is $44.99 a year. Our [Clearspace review](/blog/clearspace-app-review/) goes through the details.

### 5. one sec: the pause

one sec adds a deliberate delay and a question before an app opens. It's the cheapest option here, with free essentials and Pro at $19.99 a year, and it's honest about being for people who already want to change. It doesn't lock anything; you can walk straight through the pause. We compare it with LockPact in [one sec vs. LockPact](/blog/onesec-vs-lockpact/).

## How do these compare to Brick on strength?

Honestly: none of them match a physical device for friction, and any app that claims otherwise is overselling. An iPhone owner can always turn off an app's Screen Time access in Settings, and Apple gives apps no way to stop that. What an app can do is make leaving slower, louder or someone else's decision.

That sorts the five into four kinds of friction: a delay (one sec), a task (Clearspace), a stricter mode you control (Opal, AppBlock), and a second person (LockPact). Brick is a fifth kind: a place you have to go. Choose by which kind you'd actually respect at 11pm.
