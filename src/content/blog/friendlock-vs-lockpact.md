FriendLock shows up in searches for "friend controls your screen time" apps, and the name invites the comparison to LockPact directly. Worth being precise about it, though: FriendLock is a real, current app — live on the App Store, developed by Mitness Limited, 4.8 stars off 27 ratings as of this writing — but its "friend" role isn't what the name suggests. There's no approval, no gatekeeping, no one saying yes or no to your request. It's a different mechanic entirely, and the two apps end up solving fairly different problems — see our full roundup of [apps that let a friend control your screen time](/blog/apps-that-let-a-friend-control-your-screen-time/) for how it stacks up against the ones that actually gatekeep.

## How FriendLock actually works

You pick apps to limit — social media, games, whatever pulls you in. Instead of asking a friend for time when you hit your limit, you earn time upfront: take a selfie with a friend, and you get one hour of usable screen time, bankable up to three hours. Once you've earned it, it's yours — no approval step, no friend reviewing anything, no request that can be denied. You just spend the hour whenever you want.

It's free with an optional FriendLock Pro upgrade ($1.99/month, $14.99/year, or $49.99 lifetime) that adds unlimited time banking, daily screen time goals, and more detailed stats. It requires iOS 16 or later.

The honest way to describe FriendLock: it's a social-proof nudge, not an accountability gate. The friend's role is to be physically present for a selfie — a small, real-world action that makes you pause before doubling down on scrolling — not to exercise judgment over whether you should get more time. Once the selfie happens, your friend has no further say.

## How LockPact actually works

LockPact is built around an actual approval step. You and a partner pair up and lock apps on each other's phones. When you want back into something locked, you send an unlock request, and your partner has to actively approve or decline it — a real decision, made in the moment, that can go either way. It's mutual: whatever you've locked on their phone works the same way in reverse.

It's free with no subscription — the mutual lock-and-approve mechanic is the whole product. LockPact also detects if someone disables Screen Time access to sidestep the lock and notifies their partner when it happens.

## Where FriendLock genuinely wins

**You want a lighter touch.** FriendLock doesn't ask your friend to make a judgment call about your screen time — it just asks them to show up for a photo. If the idea of a partner actively denying your request sounds like more friction than you want, FriendLock's model is gentler by design.

**You'd rather earn than ask.** Some people respond better to a small positive action (get a selfie, get an hour) than to a request that might get turned down. FriendLock is built around earning, not asking.

**Multiple casual friends work fine.** Because there's no ongoing approval relationship, any friend willing to take a selfie can participate. You don't need one dedicated partner.

## Where LockPact wins

**You want actual accountability, not just a nudge.** If a friend being "in the loop" isn't enough and you want someone with real veto power over your screen time, FriendLock doesn't provide that — LockPact does. Once you've taken the selfie in FriendLock, nothing stops you from using the earned hour on exactly the app you were trying to avoid.

**You want it mutual.** FriendLock's selfie mechanic doesn't create an obligation running back the other way. LockPact's partner also has apps locked on their phone that you're the one approving — it's reciprocal, not one-directional.

**You want bypass detection.** LockPact watches for someone turning off Screen Time access to route around the lock and tells their partner. FriendLock's earn-and-bank model doesn't have an equivalent concept, since there's no lock to route around in the same sense.

**You want the core feature free, permanently.** FriendLock's core loop is free; LockPact's is too, and neither charges for the base experience. Where they differ is what the paid tier unlocks — FriendLock's Pro adds stats and unlimited banking, features that don't exist in LockPact because there's nothing analogous to bank.

## Choose them if / choose us if

**Choose FriendLock if:** you want a low-friction, positive nudge rather than a gatekeeper, you're comfortable earning time that no one can later deny you, and you like the idea of casual friends being involved without a formal partnership.

**Choose LockPact if:** you want a friend or partner who can actually say no, you want the relationship to be mutual instead of one-directional, and you want the app to notice if you try to route around the lock entirely.

These aren't really competing for the same use case once you look past the "friend is involved" headline — FriendLock is a habit nudge, LockPact is a commitment device. If you're only looking for a gentle push, FriendLock is honestly a fine, cheap option. If you're looking for something with teeth, LockPact is built for that.
